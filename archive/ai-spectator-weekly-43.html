The AI Spectator, November 6, 2026

A 1,147-page
proof goes
to a committee.

OpenAI says its language model proved the existence of quantum Yang-Mills theory. Physicist David Gross assembled a committee to check it. The committee says it owes OpenAI nothing, and says the mass gap is still open.

By David Borish November 6, 2026

On October 16, OpenAI asked physicist David Gross to take custody of a proof of quantum Yang-Mills existence. Gross chose four colleagues to form a committee: Michael Douglas, Arthur Jaffe, Igor Klebanov and Nikita Nekrasov. Their two-page statement describes a 1,147-page proof produced by OpenAI’s language model.

The statement says the committee has no responsibility to OpenAI. Its stated purpose is narrow: avoid releasing wrong results, and avoid leaving out attributions. The arrangement puts a check between the claim and the public record, which is the point of it.

The result covers Yang-Mills theory with an SU(3) gauge group coupled to fermions, the basis of quantum chromodynamics. The statement credits Kenneth Wilson’s 1974 lattice regularization, renormalization group methods, and Tadeusz Balaban’s work from the 1980s.

What the result does not claim matters as much. The statement says confinement and the mass gap remain open. That bears on the Clay Millennium Prize, whose formulation requires a mass gap. News reports say OpenAI claims only existence, not the mass gap.

The proof sits inside a run of announcements. On September 8, OpenAI announced a Navier-Stokes result with a Lean formalization. Clay has not accepted it, and commentators questioned whether it addressed the forced or the unforced equations. A natural-language Yang-Mills existence proof is reported for September 9 and a QCD proof for September 18. The Lean formalization of the Yang-Mills result was finished on October 16, the same day Gross was approached. News reports date OpenAI’s announcement to October 26.

The committee will first check that the definitions and problem statement match standard physics and mathematics, and that the Lean formalization matches the informal argument. It will then release the Lean proof, study applications in quantum field theory with outside experts, and publish its assessment with the full manuscript. No release dates are given. The statement lists a contact address, YANGMILLS4d@gmail.com.

Until the committee reports, this is a claim and not an established result. For the problem itself, the statement points to Douglas’s review, “The Yang-Mills Millennium problem,” in Nature Reviews Physics 8, 86-97 (2026), and to the Jaffe and Witten problem description hosted by the Clay Mathematics Institute.

The committee exists to avoid releasing wrong results or omitting attributions. No dates are given for the Lean proof, the manuscript or the assessment. The AI Spectator

Video Models · Open Weights No II

Five times faster,
by the company’s own count.

The Biological Computing Co. released Mischief 1 on October 30, an image-to-video model built on Alibaba’s open-weight Wan 2.2. The speed claim has no independent benchmark behind it yet.

The Biological Computing Co., a San Francisco startup known as TBC, released Mischief 1 on October 30. It is an image-to-video model built on Alibaba’s open-weight Wan 2.2. On fal it costs $0.005 per second at 480p and $0.01 per second at 720p, so a five-second 480p clip costs about 2.5 cents.

TBC says Mischief 1 generates video five times faster than Wan 2.2 at matching quality, at 80 percent lower inference cost. Those are the same claim in different units. The press release gives no hardware, resolution or quality metric, and Unite.AI noted that no independent benchmark exists.

The numbers that do exist point the same way. TBC’s product page shows average times of 142 seconds for Wan 2.2 and 12 seconds for Mischief 1, roughly twelve to one, though real times vary. OpenRouter shows a median latency of 12.73 seconds for Mischief 1 and no Wan 2.2 comparison. TBC told The AI Innovator that 100 five-second clips took about six minutes.

Customer and outside evidence is thin. Wonder Studios co-founder Justin Hackney said speed and cost let the studio do more within the same time and budget. A July test by Bluesky Compute reported 4.4 times lower inference cost and more than three times as much coherent video, with few details published.

The neuron component is what sets the company apart. TBC grows cortical neurons on chips with 4,096 electrodes. Its Neural Dynamics Adapter encodes a rule that activity stays local and decays, which helps keep neighboring image regions coherent. The adapter adds under 0.1 percent to base model parameters. TBC’s FAQ says no living cells are involved in video generation, and co-founder Jon Pomeraniec said the neurons stay completely in the lab.

The adapter’s results come from TBC’s own blog. On Oasis, a Minecraft world model of about 600 million parameters, an adapter of about 156,000 parameters scored about 19 percent better than the original on differential entropy across ten test videos. It beat same-size fine-tuning by 15 percent and LoRA by 5 percent. A follow-up raised frame rate from about two to just under ten per second.

What is missing is the link between that result and Mischief 1. Nothing public shows the benefit on Wan 2.2, and The Decoder noted TBC has not published a controlled comparison against distillation or caching on the same base model. Per The AI Innovator, the neurons are commercially sourced rat neurons and neurons from human stem cells. Ethics debate in the field centers on human-derived cultures, and none of the commentary cited in the article mentions TBC.

TBC says it is applying the process to newer open-weight models, with no figures yet, and an AWS Marketplace listing is coming soon. A company can build on published weights and sell a faster version of them. For the cost case behind open weights, see Open-Prem Inflection Point V3.

[ WEIGHT-SPACE LOG :: NOVEMBER 2, 2026 ]

A language model
read the weights
and rewrote them.

$ astra --input kinetix_ramp_policy --weights 171664 --tools none --prompts 1
> baseline: PPO, 1B training steps, 0 of 256 evaluation episodes succeeded
> edit: 3.5% of weights changed, small side path added to back the car up
> no gradient descent, no training loop
$ evaluate --episodes 1024
> result: 1024 of 1024 episodes succeeded

On November 2, Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab published a blog post on Weight-Space Programming. A language model, GPT-6 Astra, reasons about a network’s weights and edits them directly, with no gradient descent and no training loop. The original post and public logs are online.

The headline result is a PPO policy trained for 1 billion steps on the Kinetix car-on-ramp task. It never succeeded in 256 evaluation episodes. Given its 171,664 weights and one prompt, with no tools, Astra changed 3.5 percent of them and added a small side path that backs the car up. The edited policy succeeded in all 1,024 test episodes.

Reading weights. Astra identified the game behind each of four small MLPs of 17,253 weights, trained on Breakout, Pong, Pac-Man and Freeway. It identified the shape each of three diffusion models generated, from the weights alone. Given the middle blocks of three 7.2M-parameter character-level transformers, it identified the languages (Python, romanized Japanese, English) and the copying heads. It failed a sycophancy test on two Gemma 3 270M fine-tunes from weights alone in four attempts. With an attention-pattern tool, capped at 20 runs, it picked the sycophantic model in all three pairs.

Writing weights from zeros. Astra received game rules, source code and layout, but no data, gradients or rollouts. On MinAtar Breakout, PPO scored 15.8 at 100M steps and 25.3 at 500M. Astra’s one-shot network scored 517.4. The picture is mixed elsewhere. On Seaquest, PPO scored 5.6 and 88.9 against Astra’s 53.1. On Asterix, PPO scored 25.8 and 55.2 against Astra’s 53.2.

On 9x9 Go, a 1,480,274-weight network was too large for Astra’s context, so it was built from a 336-line script. It won over half its games against two smaller PPO networks. With 50 forward passes, Astra produced a network that beat an equal-sized PPO network trained on 20 million games.

Editing a language model. The unedited Qwen3.5-0.8B hit the 32,768-token limit on 93 percent of MATH-500 answers, mostly from reasoning loops. Astra traced the loops to one direction written by MLPs in layers 15 and 19 and removed it from 64 output columns in each, changing 131,072 weights. Baseline scores were 7.9 percent on MATH-500, 13.8 on GSM8K and 21.0 on IFEval. After 10 passes they were 12.3, 25.7 and 49.0, and token-limit hits fell to 87.2 percent. After 25 passes: 14.1, 25.1 and 39.9. After 50 passes: 48.1, 67.1 and 62.3, with token-limit hits at 33.1 percent. Scores did not rise steadily with budget.

On Gemma 3 270M, which loops on 8.5 percent of answers, MATH-500 went from 6.8 percent to at most 7.7 percent. The authors conclude that weight editing can remove an obstacle but cannot add an ability.

The authors call the results very early and cite context length and harness design as constraints. They are building a benchmark, and they propose auditing networks for backdoors and steering capabilities without data. The post does not test that proposal. Editing weights requires holding them, which ties the method to models a user can download and run. For that side of the argument, see Open-Prem Inflection Point V3.

World Models · Evaluation No IV

Odyssey-3 Scores 66.1
on Physics-IQ,
With Conditions Attached

§ § §

Odyssey’s best video-to-video score is a best-of-eight result with prompt enhancement at 1280x720. A single sample scores 63.4. The company has opened a free research preview and has disclosed no revenue or customers.

Odyssey-3 Pro scored 66.1 on the video-to-video track of Physics-IQ Verified, according to the lab’s October 8 launch post. The score carries conditions. It is a best-of-eight result, with prompt enhancement, at 1280x720. A single sample with prompt enhancement scores 63.4.

The base Odyssey-3 at 832x480 shows how much the conditions matter. It scores 51.8 with unmodified prompts, 61.6 with prompt enhancement and 64.4 as best-of-eight. Prompt enhancement alone accounts for nearly ten points. On the image-to-video track, Pro reached 54.7.

On WorldMark, which uses 13 metrics, Odyssey-3 ranked first in three of four splits. In first-person stylized it leads LingBot-World, 77.2 to 77.0. In third-person real it leads HY-World 1.5, 79.0 to 76.9. In first-person real its 80.6 places third, behind Lyra 2.0 at 84.4 and AlayaWorld at 83.0.

Odyssey-3 is an autoregressive video diffusion transformer, distilled for real-time use. Odyssey-2 streams at 20 frames per second. Starchild-1 generates joint audio and video at up to 24 frames per second.

The robotics and driving results were built on a frozen world model with a learned action decoder. A policy trained on 20 hours of simulated driving covered about 77 percent as much distance between safety-driver interventions as policies trained on real footage. Flexion used the model for whole-body humanoid control, and Odyssey reports no numeric scores for the robotics results.

PROWL-2, also covered in the article, reports 70.4 percent success on the Gate-3 quadruped task, against 29.8 percent for the SeqWM baseline. The article cites the PROWL-1 paper as arXiv 2605.18803.

On the business side, Odyssey closed a Series B on June 17, led by Natural Capital. TechCrunch puts total funding at $337 million. Odyssey’s cost estimates assume $1 per MI355X GPU-hour. The company has not disclosed revenue or customers, and the launch page lists no product pricing.

A free research preview is open at experience.odyssey.systems, and API access requests go through developer.odyssey.ml. The score is real and conditional. Comparisons with other systems should use the same sampling, resolution and prompt settings.

Health · Neurotechnology · Regulation No V

Blindsight is
waiting on
regulators.

Elon Musk says Blindsight is ready from an engineering standpoint. Neuralink’s only announced regulatory step does not authorize marketing, and no human vision results have been published.

Elon Musk wrote on X on October 29 and 30 that Blindsight is “ready from an engineering standpoint,” with the company waiting on regulatory approval. Neuralink’s only announced regulatory step is FDA Breakthrough Device Designation, disclosed September 17, 2024. That designation does not authorize marketing. Musk’s 2025 timelines for a first human vision implant have passed without published human results.

Blindsight sends camera-generated visual information to the visual cortex. Early images would be low resolution, and Musk compared them to Atari graphics at a 2025 town hall. No resolution figures have been published. Musk said one monkey carried an implant for three years. Neuralink’s electrode count was reported to grow from 1,024 to about 3,000.

The speech work moved in October. Neuralink held a 72-hour hackathon with a speech restoration participant. The work included automatic detection of speech intent, iPhone call integration, and a prototype that starts a Grok task with “Hey Neuralink.” One ALS participant reportedly reached 75 words per minute in the first week. A January update cited typing speeds up to 40 words per minute for some participants. Neuralink’s updates page and the VOICE speech study (NCT07224256) are the primary sources.

The R1 robot places electrode threads using eight cameras and optical coherence tomography scanners, and it passes through the dura mater. Reported insertion time fell from 17 seconds per thread to 1.5 seconds. About 20 people had received implants by late 2025 or early 2026.

Other groups have published on cortical vision implants. Cortigent’s Orion, with 60 electrodes, was implanted in six patients from 2018 to 2019. In January 2026 the company reported that all six performed better with the system on. Two had device- or procedure-related adverse events.

A Spanish group implanted 96-channel arrays in four blind volunteers for six months, and some participants identified letters and object boundaries. A 2021 Brain Stimulation study found that eye movements shift where evoked phosphenes appear.

Against that record, the open items are specific. Neuralink has published no resolution figures and no human Blindsight results, and its announced regulatory step is a designation, not an approval.
